Too much research never becomes a product, a service or a business.
Players across the innovation system work in isolation. Effort is duplicated, opportunities are lost and scarce resources are wasted, while innovations that already exist go unused.
Fragmented innovation ecosystems
Players across local, provincial, national and continental systems of innovation do not talk to each other. Effort is duplicated, opportunities are lost and scarce resources are wasted.
Poor translation of research into market value
Research outputs and technologies are developed at great cost but struggle to find even a first customer. Without a first customer, there is no second.
Limited commercialisation of publicly funded research
Intellectual property sits in university technology transfer offices and research institutions without reaching the market, so public investment does not make its intended impact.
Low adoption of existing innovations
Innovations that could solve problems already exist, but are not adopted or used because of limited awareness, affordability, access to finance, skills, trust and weak links to users.
A “finish, publish, graduate” culture
Postgraduate students focus on completing, publishing and graduating. Few are equipped to think about IP management, commercialisation roadmaps or the market potential of their research.
Innovation is rarely pointed at social challenges
Social investment budgets are seldom used to build lasting, innovation-led solutions.
One system, anchored on research
NiCER champions the Innovation–Commercialization–Entrepreneurship (ICE) Nexus. When the three work together, research becomes a driver of development.
Innovation
Turning knowledge into new or improved products, services, processes and models that solve real problems.
Commercialisation
Taking innovations to market through company adoption, licensing or new ventures, so that they create economic and social value.
Entrepreneurship
Building enterprises, and entrepreneurial mindsets, that carry innovations forward, create jobs and sustain impact.
Research, the anchor
Applied research and knowledge creation, including publicly funded research and indigenous knowledge systems (IKS), are the foundation from which innovation grows.
NiCER is founded on the premise that sustainable development emerges when the ICE Nexus functions as a coherent, mutually reinforcing system, anchored in applied research and knowledge creation and oriented toward real-world value creation.

About NiCER
A research-anchored developmental platform that turns knowledge into innovation, commercial value and entrepreneurial outcomes.
NiCER champions innovation, commercialisation and entrepreneurship as one integrated system.
NiCER (Network for Innovation, Commercialization and Entrepreneurship anchored on Research) is a non-profit organisation established to champion the Innovation–Commercialization–Entrepreneurship (ICE) Nexus as an integrated system for advancing sustainable economic and societal development.
NiCER operates as a research-anchored developmental platform designed to enable the systematic translation of knowledge into innovation, commercial value and entrepreneurial outcomes. Rather than supporting isolated interventions, NiCER strengthens the connective tissue between research, markets and enterprise creation.
The organisation works across sectors and geographies. It engages researchers, innovators, entrepreneurs, industry, policymakers and funders within and beyond national boundaries to improve ecosystem coherence and long-term impact.

When these elements are pursued in silos, the result is research that stays on the shelf, start-ups without technology and companies without access to new knowledge.

NiCER is founded on the premise that sustainable development emerges when the ICE Nexus functions as a coherent, mutually reinforcing system, anchored in applied research and knowledge creation and oriented toward real-world value creation. The little resources that exist in the ecosystem must be optimised, not duplicated.
Vision
A world in which research-driven innovation is systematically transformed into sustainable economic and societal value.
Mission
To champion innovation for sustainable development in Africa, by championing the Innovation–Commercialization–Entrepreneurship (ICE) Nexus and enabling the translation of research outcomes into innovative solutions, sustainable enterprises and long-term developmental impact.
Our values
Inclusion
Opening the innovation ecosystem to students, communities, emerging entrepreneurs, people with disabilities and under-served regions, not only established players.
Network logic
Creating value through connections: linking players, sharing information and pooling resources rather than duplicating them.
Sector integration
Working across industry, academia, government, funders and civil society, and across industries, so that solutions travel.
Research anchoring
Grounding innovation in evidence, applied research and existing knowledge, including indigenous knowledge systems.
Collective leadership
Leading with partners rather than over them, and sharing credit, decisions and benefits fairly.
Outcome orientation
Measuring success by what reaches the market and society: adopted solutions, new ventures, skilled people and improved lives.
Our role in the ecosystem
NiCER is a champion of the ICE Nexus, a convener and connector, a builder of capability, a generator of insight, and a developmental intermediary between research, markets and entrepreneurship. In practice, that means six operational roles.
Innovation platform
A single entry point where industry, universities, students, government and funders meet, share information and co-invest.
Convener and matchmaker
Brings partners to one table and matches industry problems with students, research and intellectual property.
Partnership custodian
Signs memoranda of understanding (MOUs) with partners and holds the IP and confidentiality framework.
Programme manager
Runs stage gates and tracks progress of projects through the technology readiness levels (TRLs).
Ecosystem coordinator
Aligns sector commissions, incubators, accelerators and funders to avoid duplication.
Talent pipeline
Feeds participants from capability-building programmes into technology development and venture projects.
How NiCER addresses fragmentation
Fragmentation means duplication and waste, and it also means innovations that already exist never reach the people who need them. NiCER responds on two fronts: connecting and coordinating ecosystem players, and encouraging the adoption of innovation.
Duplication
One shared pipeline of problems, projects and opportunities, made visible through the NiCER Notice Board and communities of practice.
Lost opportunities
Industry needs systematically matched to research, students and existing IP.
Wastage
Pooled funding, facilities and expertise across partners.
Under-used innovation
Making proven solutions visible, connecting them with potential users, and helping to remove barriers to uptake such as awareness, affordability, finance, skills and trust.

iCE-Cold Networking Ecosystem
Connects researchers, innovators, entrepreneurs, industry, policymakers and funders so they coordinate instead of duplicating each other.
Objective 1 Programme 6.1
Register your interest
Objective 1: To integrate the innovation ecosystem
This programme convenes and strengthens relationships among researchers, innovators, entrepreneurs, industry partners, policymakers and funders. Its purpose is to reduce fragmentation and strengthen system-level coherence, so that ecosystem players coordinate, share best practice and avoid duplicating each other’s efforts.
Why it matters
Fragmentation is a root cause of the ecosystem’s weak contribution to socio-economic development. When players do not coordinate, they duplicate effort and waste the limited resources available. This objective is why “Network” is in our name.
What we do
We convene ecosystem players, give them platforms to present their work and best practice, and actively link and coordinate them. We consolidate ecosystem information in one place through the NiCER Notice Board.
Intended outcomes
Functional collaboration networks across the ICE Nexus.
Less duplication of effort and better use of available resources.
Faster flow of information, opportunities and partnerships.

Innovation for Social Investment
Partners with corporate social investment programmes to deliver innovative projects in sectors such as education and disability inclusion.
Objective 5 Programme 6.5
Register your interest
Objective 5: To mobilise innovation for social investment
Many companies allocate corporate social investment (CSI) and corporate social responsibility (CSR) funding every year. This programme partners with those companies to direct that funding through innovation: designing and delivering innovative, sustainable projects in social sectors such as education and support for people with disabilities, among others.
Why it matters
Companies invest significant CSI and CSR budgets every year, yet these are seldom used to build lasting, innovation-led solutions. Directing them through innovation multiplies their social return.
What we do
We partner with companies that have CSI or CSR funding to design and deliver innovative projects in social sectors such as education and support for people with disabilities. The aim is to embed innovation as a long-term capability and cultural norm in how society addresses its developmental challenges.
Intended outcomes
Innovative, sustainable solutions to social challenges.
Measurable social impact for CSI funders.
Innovation embedded as a norm in how society responds to its challenges.

Innovation to Market Accelerator
Innovation to Market Accelerator
Scales innovations and encourages the adoption of existing ones, using the LIMFO framework and fair, equitable IP management.
Objective 4 Programme 6.4
Take the psychometric test
Objective 4: To accelerate innovation to market and use
“IMA” means to stand up in Tshivenda. The programme takes innovations that have reached TRL 9, including those coming out of the NiCER Lab, and accelerates their scaling and rate of adoption in the market. It also helps existing businesses improve and scale through innovation and collaboration. Its guiding phrase is: from public research to public value.
Why it matters
Even mature technologies (TRL 9) often fail to scale. Market adoption requires leadership, a strong offering, market access, finance and an organisation to carry it, together with fair arrangements among everyone who contributed. At the same time, many innovations that already exist are not being adopted and used because of various barriers.
What we do
We help innovations and ventures scale and accelerate their adoption in the market using our LIMFO framework, supported by fair and equitable IP management and investment partnerships. We also encourage the utilisation and adoption of existing innovations, by connecting them with the people and organisations that need them and helping to remove barriers to uptake.
Intended outcomes
Commercialised products, services and ventures.
Investment-ready start-ups and growing businesses.
Greater utilisation and adoption of existing innovations.
Fair returns for investors, inventors, universities and other stakeholders.

Encouraging the adoption and use of existing innovations
Many innovations that could solve problems and contribute to sustainable development already exist, but are not being adopted and used. The IMA Program works to change this by:
Identifying proven innovations, including those surfaced by the Sector-innovations Development Units, that are under-used.
Making them visible to potential users through the NiCER network and Notice Board.
Connecting them with the companies, government departments and communities that need them.
Helping to remove barriers to uptake, such as awareness, affordability, access to finance, implementation skills and trust in new solutions.
The LIMFO framework
IMA support is structured around five domains.
L Leadership
The people leading the venture
Does the venture have the right founders, management and governance? What leadership capability needs to be built?
I Innovation
The product, service or technology
Is the innovation proven, differentiated and protected? What further development is needed?
M Market penetration
Customers and adoption
Who is the first customer, and who comes next? How will the innovation reach and win its market?
F Finance
Funding and valuation
How will the venture raise funds? How big is the opportunity, and what is the venture worth?
O Organisational development
Building the company
How do we build an organisation around the innovation and spin it out as a company? What systems, structures and people are needed?
Fair and equitable IP management
Because many stakeholders contribute to an innovation (researchers, students, universities, companies, entrepreneurs and investors), IP management is central to both the Lab and the Accelerator. NiCER’s approach is that investors must be able to earn a return, and that every stakeholder who made an input must receive a fair output. IP ownership, licensing and benefit-sharing arrangements are agreed upfront, in MOUs, before work begins.
Routes to market
Typical support, tailored to each project and set out in its commercialisation roadmap, includes market validation, certification and regulatory approval, scale-up and manufacturing, investment readiness and funding, and commercial agreements. Ventures are supported along the full private equity value chain, from investment readiness to investor exit.
Company adoption
The sponsoring or partner company deploys and scales the solution in its own operations.
Licensing
The university or IP holder licenses the IP to the sponsor or to other companies.
Spin-out venture
Students, researchers or placed entrepreneurs form a company, backed by incubators and investors.

Objective 2 Programme 6.2

Objective 2: To build innovation capability
The NiCER Academy develops capability across the innovation ecosystem. It offers accessible courses and workshops on practical concepts relating to the ICE Nexus and will run as a standalone learning platform at academy.nicer.org.
Why it matters
Most research outputs are not commercialised, partly because researchers, students and professionals have never been taught how. Capability is the foundation for every other objective.
What we do
We offer workshops and courses, many industry-led and some accredited for CPD points or by SETAs, on research commercialisation, IP management, commercialisation roadmaps, innovation and entrepreneurship. We deliver them with industry, professional bodies and academic institutions.
Intended outcomes
Commercialisation-ready postgraduate students and graduates.
Professionals and officials who understand innovation concepts.
Companies able to meet skills development targets through relevant, accredited learning.

Flagship course: research commercialisation for postgraduate students
Postgraduate students are typically focused on finishing, publishing and graduating. This course trains them to also think about how their research could be commercialised. Topics include:

How learning is delivered
Industry-led workshops
Companies working in a particular field develop and deliver workshops, which can be recorded and uploaded to the platform.
CPD-accredited learning

SETA-accredited courses

Formats
Live workshops, pre-recorded courses, masterclasses and cohort-based programmes.

Companies are required to invest a share of their payroll in skills development and many have skills development targets to meet, through both accredited and non-accredited learning. The Academy offers companies a ready catalogue of relevant courses, pre-recorded or coordinated by NiCER, to help them spend their skills development budgets meaningfully and meet their targets.
This is also a source of sustainable income for NiCER.

Research to Innovation Lab
Research to Innovation Lab
Raises the technology readiness of research outputs and IP from TRL 1 to TRL 9, starting from existing research or from industry problems.
Objective 3 Programme 6.3
Register your interest
Objective 3: To advance research to innovation and technology development
Universities, technology transfer offices (TTOs) and research institutions such as the CSIR hold a large body of intellectual property generated by research. Much of this research is publicly funded, yet the IP is not commercialised and does not make its intended impact on society. At the same time, companies face problems that research could solve. The NiCER Lab exists to close this gap.
Why it matters
Publicly funded research and IP often sit unused in universities and research institutions, while companies struggle to find solutions to their problems. Bridging the two creates technologies with a ready first customer.
What we do
We increase the technology readiness levels of research outputs from TRL 1 up to TRL 9 through a supply-driven pathway (starting from existing IP) and a demand-driven pathway (starting from industry-sponsored problems). Sector-innovations Development Units (SiDUs) use an open innovation model to link the Lab to the R&D departments of companies in various sectors.
Intended outcomes
Validated prototypes and technologies that companies adopt.
Postgraduate research that is industry-relevant, with students earning qualifications through real projects.
Spin-out ventures built around university IP.
The technology readiness level framework
The Lab is deliberate and structured: its focus is to take research outputs and increase their technology readiness level from TRL 1 all the way to TRL 9.
1 Basic principles observed
2 Technology concept formulated
3 Experimental proof of concept
4 Technology validated in the lab
5 Validated in a relevant environment
6 Demonstrated in a relevant environment
7 System prototype in an operational environment
8 System complete and qualified
9 Proven in an operational environment
Stage gates sit at TRL 3–4, 5–6 and 7–8. At TRL 9 the technology is ready to scale through the Innovation to Market Accelerator.
Two pathways into the Lab
Supply-driven pathway
Demand-driven pathway
Starting point
Existing IP or research output sitting in a university, TTO or research institution.
A real problem from a company’s R&D department.
How it works
NiCER identifies promising IP and runs a programme around it: for example, placing an entrepreneur with the research team (with equity in a spin-out venture) and supporting the technology’s development to establish whether it can be commercialised.
NiCER plugs into company R&D as an open research and innovation channel. Companies share and sponsor key problems; NiCER sources postgraduate students interested in the field and existing IP that can contribute to a solution.
Development
Technology development, with students able to earn their qualifications through the work.
Co-development supervised jointly by the industry partner and an academic partner.
Example
An indigenous knowledge systems (IKS) based product, developed in a university food sciences department, that is struggling to reach the market.
An industry-sponsored problem in one of five priority streams.
Key advantage
Unlocks public investment in research that would otherwise stay on the shelf.
The sponsoring company is the first customer, making adoption far easier. Once it works for the first client, it can be scaled to others in the same industry.
Sector-innovations Development Units
SiDUs sit within the Lab and are its sector-facing structure. Each unit focuses on one sector and uses an open innovation model to link NiCER, universities and postgraduate students to the R&D departments of companies in that sector.
IKS Agriculture Environment and mining Chemicals and manufacturing Public health and pharmaceuticals Education Digital technologies
Each SiDU
Builds relationships with the R&D departments of companies in its sector and sources industry problems for sponsorship.
Identifies the research groups, postgraduate students and existing IP that are relevant to the sector.
Drives the development of sector innovations: taking research outputs and IP through the technology readiness levels into solutions that companies in the sector can adopt.
Identifies existing innovations in the sector that are under-used, and refers them to the IMA Program for adoption support.
The demand-driven model
Developed with Sasol as the second phase of a three-phase value chain. Five priority streams, with two sponsor companies in each, give ten sponsored industry problems per cycle.
Agriculture and IKS
Climate-resilient crops and smart farming; IKS-based bio-products; agro-processing and post-harvest.
Watch-outs Prior informed consent and benefit-sharing with indigenous knowledge holders.
Environmental and mining
Water treatment and reuse; tailings and waste valorisation; rehabilitation and emissions monitoring.
Watch-outs Environmental permits and on-site access for pilots.
Chemical and manufacturing
Process efficiency and green chemistry; advanced materials; circular and recycled feedstocks.
Watch-outs Pilot plant access and process safety.
Health and pharmaceutical
Diagnostics and medical devices; IKS-derived and generic medicines; formulation and drug delivery.
Watch-outs Regulatory approval and clinical timelines can run past TRL 8.
Digital technologies
AI and data analytics; IoT and sensors; digital twins and automation.
Watch-outs Data access and privacy. Digital also enables the other four streams.
Matching students to problems
Postgraduate students are matched to each problem through four routes:
Students who have relevant skills from their area of specialisation.
Students whose research is already aligned with the problem.
Students who know of suitable IP.
New students willing to take the problem on as their research topic.
The process
Problem sourcing
Two sponsor companies per stream; each defines a problem with scope, success criteria, budget and an industry mentor.
Matching and co-development
Students matched through the four routes; joint academic and industry supervision; stage gates at TRL 3–4, 5–6 and 7–8.
Pitching and validation
Buy-in from the sponsoring company; a pilot in a company setting (TRL 7–8); a transfer package with the prototype, data and IP documentation.
Adoption and uptake
The company adopts the solution or develops it further; results are showcased to other firms; the commercialisation roadmap is ready for the next phase.
After-care
Technical support after handover, follow-on problems and lessons fed into the next cycle.
Value created
Companies
Tested solutions to real problems and low-cost R&D capacity.
Students
Industry-relevant research, qualifications and employment prospects.
Universities
Commercialised IP, income and stronger industry links.
The economy
Locally developed technology and new ventures.
