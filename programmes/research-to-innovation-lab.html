Research to Innovation Lab

Raises the technology readiness of research outputs and IP from TRL 1 to TRL 9, starting from existing research or from industry problems.

Objective 3Programme 6.3

Objective 3: To advance research to innovation and technology development

Universities, technology transfer offices (TTOs) and research institutions such as the CSIR hold a large body of intellectual property generated by research. Much of this research is publicly funded, yet the IP is not commercialised and does not make its intended impact on society. At the same time, companies face problems that research could solve. The NiCER Lab exists to close this gap.

Why it matters

Publicly funded research and IP often sit unused in universities and research institutions, while companies struggle to find solutions to their problems. Bridging the two creates technologies with a ready first customer.

What we do

We increase the technology readiness levels of research outputs from TRL 1 up to TRL 9 through a supply-driven pathway (starting from existing IP) and a demand-driven pathway (starting from industry-sponsored problems). Sector-innovations Development Units (SiDUs) use an open innovation model to link the Lab to the R&D departments of companies in various sectors.

Intended outcomes

  • Validated prototypes and technologies that companies adopt.
  • Postgraduate research that is industry-relevant, with students earning qualifications through real projects.
  • Spin-out ventures built around university IP.

The technology readiness level framework

The Lab is deliberate and structured: its focus is to take research outputs and increase their technology readiness level from TRL 1 all the way to TRL 9.

1Basic principles observed
2Technology concept formulated
3Experimental proof of concept
4Technology validated in the lab
5Validated in a relevant environment
6Demonstrated in a relevant environment
7System prototype in an operational environment
8System complete and qualified
9Proven in an operational environment

Stage gates sit at TRL 3–4, 5–6 and 7–8. At TRL 9 the technology is ready to scale through the Innovation to Market Accelerator.

Two pathways into the Lab

Supply-driven pathway
Demand-driven pathway
Starting point
Existing IP or research output sitting in a university, TTO or research institution.
A real problem from a company’s R&D department.
How it works
NiCER identifies promising IP and runs a programme around it: for example, placing an entrepreneur with the research team (with equity in a spin-out venture) and supporting the technology’s development to establish whether it can be commercialised.
NiCER plugs into company R&D as an open research and innovation channel. Companies share and sponsor key problems; NiCER sources postgraduate students interested in the field and existing IP that can contribute to a solution.
Development
Technology development, with students able to earn their qualifications through the work.
Co-development supervised jointly by the industry partner and an academic partner.
Example
An indigenous knowledge systems (IKS) based product, developed in a university food sciences department, that is struggling to reach the market.
An industry-sponsored problem in one of five priority streams.
Key advantage
Unlocks public investment in research that would otherwise stay on the shelf.
The sponsoring company is the first customer, making adoption far easier. Once it works for the first client, it can be scaled to others in the same industry.

Sector-innovations Development Units

SiDUs sit within the Lab and are its sector-facing structure. Each unit focuses on one sector and uses an open innovation model to link NiCER, universities and postgraduate students to the R&D departments of companies in that sector.

IKSAgricultureEnvironment and miningChemicals and manufacturingPublic health and pharmaceuticalsEducationDigital technologies

Each SiDU

  • Builds relationships with the R&D departments of companies in its sector and sources industry problems for sponsorship.
  • Identifies the research groups, postgraduate students and existing IP that are relevant to the sector.
  • Drives the development of sector innovations: taking research outputs and IP through the technology readiness levels into solutions that companies in the sector can adopt.
  • Identifies existing innovations in the sector that are under-used, and refers them to the IMA Program for adoption support.

The demand-driven model

Developed with Sasol as the second phase of a three-phase value chain. Five priority streams, with two sponsor companies in each, give ten sponsored industry problems per cycle.

Agriculture and IKS

Climate-resilient crops and smart farming; IKS-based bio-products; agro-processing and post-harvest.

Watch-outsPrior informed consent and benefit-sharing with indigenous knowledge holders.

Environmental and mining

Water treatment and reuse; tailings and waste valorisation; rehabilitation and emissions monitoring.

Watch-outsEnvironmental permits and on-site access for pilots.

Chemical and manufacturing

Process efficiency and green chemistry; advanced materials; circular and recycled feedstocks.

Watch-outsPilot plant access and process safety.

Health and pharmaceutical

Diagnostics and medical devices; IKS-derived and generic medicines; formulation and drug delivery.

Watch-outsRegulatory approval and clinical timelines can run past TRL 8.

Digital technologies

AI and data analytics; IoT and sensors; digital twins and automation.

Watch-outsData access and privacy. Digital also enables the other four streams.

Matching students to problems

Postgraduate students are matched to each problem through four routes:

  • Students who have relevant skills from their area of specialisation.
  • Students whose research is already aligned with the problem.
  • Students who know of suitable IP.
  • New students willing to take the problem on as their research topic.

The process

Problem sourcing

Two sponsor companies per stream; each defines a problem with scope, success criteria, budget and an industry mentor.

Matching and co-development

Students matched through the four routes; joint academic and industry supervision; stage gates at TRL 3–4, 5–6 and 7–8.

Pitching and validation

Buy-in from the sponsoring company; a pilot in a company setting (TRL 7–8); a transfer package with the prototype, data and IP documentation.

Adoption and uptake

The company adopts the solution or develops it further; results are showcased to other firms; the commercialisation roadmap is ready for the next phase.

After-care

Technical support after handover, follow-on problems and lessons fed into the next cycle.

Value created

Companies

Tested solutions to real problems and low-cost R&D capacity.

Students

Industry-relevant research, qualifications and employment prospects.

Universities

Commercialised IP, income and stronger industry links.

The economy

Locally developed technology and new ventures.

Interventions

Sasol Research to Innovation Program (Phase 2)

2027 (planned)

Industry-sponsored problems in five priority streams: two sponsor companies per stream, ten problems per cycle, co-developed by matched postgraduate students.

Partner: Sasol (proposed); open to other partners

University IP Commercialisation

2027 (planned)

Taking publicly funded IP held in TTOs and research institutions (for example IKS-based products) to market through student projects, entrepreneur placement and spin-outs.

Partner: Open to partners

Sector-innovations Development Units (SiDUs)

2027

Sector-based units within the Lab that use an open innovation model to link to the R&D departments of companies, and drive the development of innovations in sectors including IKS, agriculture, education, public health, digital and manufacturing.

Partner: Open to partners

Vhembe Innovation and Commercialisation (VICtry) Centre

2025 to date

Championing the development of an innovation and commercialisation centre in the Vhembe district, to help operationalise the Department of Science and Innovation’s innovation-driven district development model.

Partner: Vhembe District, UNIVEN, WIBC; open to other partners
